Understanding Multiple Myeloma

What Is Multiple Myeloma?

Multiple Myeloma is a cancer of plasma cells — the antibody-producing white blood cells that live in the bone marrow. When plasma cells become malignant, they crowd out normal bone marrow function, produce abnormal proteins (M-protein) that accumulate in the blood and urine, and erode bone, leading to fractures and bone pain. The American Cancer Society estimates more than 35,000 Americans are diagnosed with Multiple Myeloma each year.

Multiple Myeloma is preceded by precursor conditions: Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance (MGUS) and Smoldering Multiple Myeloma. A Roundup case can be evaluated based on the active myeloma diagnosis even when the disease evolved through a precursor stage.

Common Symptoms (CRAB Criteria)

  • C — HyperCalcemia (elevated calcium levels)

  • R — Renal failure (kidney damage from light chains)

  • A — Anemia (low red blood cells, fatigue, shortness of breath)

  • B — Bone lesions, pathologic fractures, bone pain (often in the back, ribs, and hips)

  • Recurrent infections from suppressed immune function

  • Hyperviscosity, neuropathy, and other paraprotein-related symptoms

Plasma Cell Cancers In The Roundup MDL

  • Multiple Myeloma — Active disease, the core diagnosis.
  • Smoldering Multiple Myeloma — Active myeloma at lower disease burden, can be evaluated when it has progressed or required treatment.
  • MGUS → Multiple Myeloma — If MGUS progressed into active myeloma, the case is built on the myeloma diagnosis.
  • Plasmacytoma — A solitary mass of malignant plasma cells, in bone or in soft tissue.
  • Plasma Cell Leukemia — A rare and aggressive form of plasma cell malignancy.

The Agricultural Worker Link

Multiple peer-reviewed studies of farmers, pesticide applicators, and other agricultural workers have found increased rates of Multiple Myeloma associated with cumulative glyphosate exposure. The Agricultural Health Study (AHS) and other large cohort analyses have specifically flagged this association.

Why The Diagnosis Detail Matters

Myeloma diagnosis depends on bone marrow plasma cell percentage, M-protein on serum and urine electrophoresis, free light chain ratio, imaging for bone lesions, and the CRAB / SLiM-CRAB criteria. B-cell Origin
